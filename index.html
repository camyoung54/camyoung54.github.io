About Me


Hello! My name is Cameron Young and I am an MD/MBA candidate at Harvard Medical School and Harvard Business School. I'm committed to advancing equity in pediatric healthcare by integrating medical expertise with data science and AI, translating research into scalable solutions across clinical, academic, and startup settings.

Previously, I was a Churchill Scholar at the University of Cambridge, where I completed an MPhil degree in Medical Sciences with the Caldas Lab at the Cancer Research UK Cambridge Institute. There, I developed personalized therapeutic strategies using genomics and machine learning. I graduated from Northeastern University with dual degrees in chemical engineering and biochemistry.

Currently, I serve as a clinical consultant at Centaur Labs, shaping AI solutions for patient communication and clinical documentation. I'm also co-founder and Director of Research at Ecotera Health, an early-stage startup addressing the health impacts of microplastic pollution through home water testing and diagnostics. At Mass General Brigham Innovation, I lead the development of LLM-based tools to optimize medical decision-making and reduce provider bias, with findings published in PAIN and the American Journal of Medical Genetics.

My research has resulted in 48+ publications in high-impact journals including the New England Journal of Medicine, JAMA, Nature family journals, and others, with 3,618+ citations. I was the first recipient of the Churchill Scholarship in Northeastern University's history and was also awarded a Goldwater Scholarship.

Outside of research and entrepreneurship, I enjoy running (including the 2024 Boston Marathon!), CrossFit, exploring new cultures through travel, and attending concerts. To learn more about my work, explore the sections below or view my full CV here.

Research Experience

My research career spans multiple disciplines and institutions, focusing on translating scientific discoveries into clinical applications that improve patient outcomes.

Current Research Focus

At Mass General Brigham Innovation, I spearhead the development of novel large language model (LLM)-based tools to optimize medical decision-making and address critical issues like provider bias in healthcare. My work has resulted in publications demonstrating racial, ethnic, and sex bias in AI-driven opioid recommendations (PAIN, 2024) and improved diagnostic accuracy for rare pediatric diseases using custom LLMs integrated with the Human Phenotype Ontology (American Journal of Medical Genetics, 2025).

I've published 3+ open-source tools for translation into clinical practice, bridging the gap between cutting-edge AI research and real-world medical applications.

Cancer Genomics & Machine Learning

As a Churchill Scholar at the Cancer Research UK Cambridge Institute, I developed CopyClust, a reliable DNA copy-number-based machine learning algorithm for breast cancer integrative cluster classification. This work, published in Scientific Reports (2024), achieved a >10% improvement in classification accuracy compared to existing gold standard methods and is now available as an open-source R package being utilized in ongoing large-scale genomics studies worldwide.

COVID-19 Pediatric Research

During the COVID-19 pandemic, I worked with Dr. Adrienne Randolph at Boston Children's Hospital in conjunction with the CDC to coordinate a \$1.5 million public health surveillance registry. This nationwide effort tracked severe pediatric COVID-19 illness and Multisystem Inflammatory Syndrome in Children (MIS-C) across 70+ hospitals, analyzing data from 10,000+ patients.

Our findings, published in premier journals including the New England Journal of Medicine, JAMA, and Nature, influenced global standards of care and informed WHO and CDC protocols for MIS-C diagnosis and treatment.

Biomedical Engineering & Drug Delivery

At MIT and Brigham and Women's Hospital, I worked with Drs. Giovanni Traverso and Robert Langer to develop personalized radiation attenuating materials for gastrointestinal mucosal protection. This novel class of devices reduced harmful radiation exposure by >30% in cancer patients and has been translated into a phase I clinical trial for head and neck cancer (Advanced Science, 2021).

I also discovered previously unknown drug-drug interactions in the gastrointestinal tract using Python-based machine learning models, with findings published in Nature Biomedical Engineering (2024) that could expedite drug development and enhance safety evaluation.

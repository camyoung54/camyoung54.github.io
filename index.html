About

I'm Cameron Young, an MD candidate at Harvard Medical School working where medicine meets AI. I build and evaluate large language model tools for clinical decision-making, and I advise organizations on putting AI to work in healthcare responsibly.

Before medical school I was a Churchill Scholar at the University of Cambridge, earning an MPhil in Medical Sciences in the Caldas Lab. I hold dual degrees in chemical engineering and biochemistry from Northeastern University, and my research spans AI, cancer genomics, pediatric COVID-19, and biomedical engineering, with 45+ publications in journals including NEJM, JAMA, and Nature family journals.

Outside of work I run (including the 2024 Boston Marathon), do CrossFit, travel, and go to as many concerts as I can.

Work

AI Consulting

I advise healthcare and life-science teams on AI strategy and implementation, from scoping use cases to evaluating LLM tools for accuracy, safety, and bias.

Clinical AI · Mass General Brigham

I lead development of LLM-based tools for medical decision-making at MGB Innovation, including work showing racial, ethnic, and sex bias in LLM opioid recommendations (PAIN, 2024) and a custom LLM for rare pediatric disease diagnosis (Am J Med Genet, 2025).

Cancer Genomics · University of Cambridge

At the Cancer Research UK Cambridge Institute I built CopyClust, an open-source machine learning classifier for breast cancer integrative clusters (Sci Rep, 2024).

Pediatric COVID-19 · Boston Children's / CDC

I coordinated a CDC-funded surveillance registry of severe pediatric COVID-19 and MIS-C across 70+ hospitals, with findings in NEJM and JAMA that informed CDC and WHO guidance.

Biomedical Engineering · MIT / Brigham

I helped develop personalized radiation-attenuating devices that advanced to a phase I trial (Adv Sci, 2021) and used machine learning to uncover drug–transporter interactions (Nat Biomed Eng, 2024).
